Teaching Notes

  
Before you teach it

  

    

      
Two ways to run it

      
As pre-work (recommended). Assign the game before the Module 2 session
      (~10 minutes) with a one-line reflection: “Which answer surprised you most, and where do
      you think your instinct came from?” Open class by collecting surprises — the discussion
      usually starts itself.

      
Live, as a class opener (15–20 min). Project the game in Projector mode.
      For each question, have students vote by show of hands, phone poll, or shouted guesses; enter
      the room's consensus on the slider and lock it in together. The reveal lands hardest when the
      class has publicly committed to a number first.

      
Care note

      
Some students are migrants, or children of migrants, and round 8 concerns displaced
      children. Keep individual guesses private in the solo version (don't ask students to share
      scores), and frame gaps as everyone's gaps — the research shows systematic
      misperception across whole publics, not individual ignorance.

    

    

      
Discussion questions

      
Tiered, matching the module's discussion format.

      
    
        
  1. Describe: Which of your guesses was furthest from the data? Was the class's direction (high/low) consistent?
    2. 
        
  2. Describe: Which number do you think you had encountered before, and where?
    3. 
        
  3. Analyze: Why do publics systematically overestimate the share of migrants — what supplies the mental images the guesses come from?
    4. 
        
  4. Analyze: Round 7 shows the migrant share rose only from 2.9% to 3.7% since 1990. Why does “an age of unprecedented mass migration” still feel true?
    5. 
        
  5. Analyze: Round 8's answer is an estimate with huge uncertainty. Who benefits, and who is harmed, when a number is missing?
    6. 
        
  6. Evaluate: The lecture's Brexit case shows the same statistic framed as crisis or fact. Pick one game figure and write both frames for it.
    7. 
        
  7. Evaluate: If perception gaps are this systematic, what obligations do journalists, politicians — and professors — have when they quote migration numbers?
